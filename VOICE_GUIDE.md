# Voice & Style Guide — The Unconscious Consumer

Distilled from the existing catalogue (e.g. *Less is More: Unpacking the Power of Scarcity
Bias*, *From Page to Practice: Thinking, Fast and Slow*, *Behind Dark Patterns*). Upload this
to the Claude Project as knowledge so new drafts sound like the existing work. For visual/brand
rules see `TUC_DESIGN_BRIEF.md`; for the editorial backlog see `CONTENT_PLAN.md`.

## Audience & stance

- **Readers:** industry peers and collaborators — UX researchers, PMs, behavioural-science
  people. They can smell self-promotion and will leave if it reads like a personal-brand play.
- **Stance:** intellectually curious and credible, never preachy or salesy. The writer is a
  cognitive psychologist / UX researcher who turns research into strategy.
- **Throughline:** nearly every piece carries an **ethical question** — is this influence
  *ingenious or manipulative?* — and ends by empowering the reader to notice the unseen forces.

## Voice characteristics

- **Accessible-academic, lightly literary.** Plain explanations of real research, lifted by the
  occasional metaphor ("the mythical sirens", "a carefully choreographed dance of design and
  deceit"). Don't overdo the metaphors — one or two per piece.
- **Concept → real case study, every time.** Abstract ideas are always anchored in named
  companies and products: Amazon 1-click, Airbnb pricing, Apple launches, Spotify, Slack,
  Grammarly. A reader should never sit with an abstraction for more than a paragraph.
- **Research-grounded.** Cite real sources inline as hyperlinks — academic (Kahneman, Cialdini,
  Iyengar, Thaler) and institutional (McKinsey, Deloitte, peer-reviewed studies). Claims lean
  on evidence, not assertion.
- **British/Canadian spelling**, always: behaviour, favour, colour, rationalise.
- **Second person and rhetorical questions** to pull the reader in ("Have you ever noticed
  yourself making a purchase that was difficult to rationalise?").

## Structure (the house template)

1. **Hook opening** — a recent news event, a discovery, or a rhetorical question. Concrete, not
   a definition. (Optional **TL;DR** bullet block up top for longer pieces.)
2. **Frame the concept** — name the principle and ground it in one vivid example.
3. **3–5 body sections** under descriptive, slightly evocative `H2`/`H3` headings
   ("The Primal Blueprint", "The Allure of Dark Patterns", "Ingenious or Manipulative?").
   Each section = one idea + one case study + at least one cited source.
4. **Actionable Recommendations** (common, not mandatory) — pair a **Behavioural Concept** with
   a concrete **Recommendation** the reader could apply.
5. **Reflective conclusion** — zoom out to the ethics and what it means for a conscious consumer.

- **Length:** ~7–10 min read (roughly 1,400–2,200 words).
- **Headings:** descriptive and evocative is fine in article bodies. (Note: the *site UI* uses
  sentence case and forbids emoji in titles — emoji in a title are stripped at render time.)

## Do / Don't

- **Do** open on something concrete; **don't** open with a dictionary definition.
- **Do** name real companies and cite real research; **don't** make unsupported claims.
- **Do** keep an even, curious tone; **don't** moralise or hype ("game-changing", "revolutionary").
- **Do** use terracotta-brand restraint in tone — serious, not promotional; **don't** add
  calls-to-subscribe inside the essay body (the site handles that).
- **Don't** use emoji in the title. **Don't** use American spellings.
