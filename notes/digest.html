Sometimes It Hurts to Ask

Published 2026-11-01
But you should probably do it anyway
Directly about the ask gap and people underestimating willingness to say yes, prime behavioural science territory.
Your angle
The reluctance to ask is a forecasting error: people systematically underestimate how often others will comply because they focus on the request and ignore the social cost of refusing. This is the same say-do gap I keep flagging, people predict their own and others' behaviour badly, and the prediction errors are patterned, not random.

Your angle
The claim that the best marketers are allergic to AI is really a claim about authenticity as a scarce signal: once AI generated UGC floods the channel, the handmade stuff gets a status premium precisely because it is costly to produce. That is signalling theory doing the work, not a marketing hunch.

Published 2026-10-27
Three theories
Derek Thompson on a cultural phenomenon with 'three theories' invites a consumer-psychology reframe.
Your angle
Thompson's three theories are worth testing against a simpler one: what people say draws them to 'you can see everything' and what actually holds their attention are two different things, and the stated reason is usually the respectable one. Revealed preference beats the focus group every time.
