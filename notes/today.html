Note 1 hook
The ad that made you tear up did not sell you a product. It rented your empathy. When you watch someone enjoy something, your brain simulates the enjoyment as if it were yours. Marketers learned this before neuroscientists named it.
From: Emotions in Motion: How Mirror Neurons Drive Consumer Behaviour
It reframes emotional advertising as neural borrowing rather than persuasion, which stops the scroll by making the reader feel implicated.
Note 2 pattern
The brands most damaged by a lie are the ones you trusted most. Betrayal scales with expectation. A discount airline that disappoints costs you nothing. A premium brand caught overstating a core promise breaks something you cannot easily rebuild.
From: Undercurrents: Tesla's Range Controversy and the Fragility of Brand Trust
It states a counterintuitive pattern (trust magnifies fallout) with a concrete contrast that makes readers stop and nod.
Note 3 example
A friend of mine buys vinyl she also streams. Same album, twice. Ask her why and she talks about the sleeve, the needle, the walk across the room. She is not paying for the music. She is paying for the friction the digital version removed.
From: Undercurrents: The Quiet Return of Analog in a Digital World
A concrete scene about one person surfaces the counterintuitive idea that friction is the product, not a flaw.
