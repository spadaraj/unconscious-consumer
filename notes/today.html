Note 1 hook
The ad that made you tear up did not sell you a product. It rented your empathy. When you watch someone enjoy something, your brain simulates the enjoyment as if it were yours. Marketers learned this before neuroscientists named it.
From: Emotions in Motion: How Mirror Neurons Drive Consumer Behaviour
It reframes emotional advertising as neural borrowing rather than persuasion, which stops the scroll by making the reader feel implicated.
Note 2 example
Kodak invented the digital camera in 1975. Then shelved it, because it threatened the film business. The instinct to protect what pays you today is exactly what kills you tomorrow.
From: Bouncing Back: The Blueprint for Fostering Business Resilience in Turbulent Times
A sharp, specific case with a counterintuitive detail (Kodak built the thing that killed it) that lands a clear POV without needing the article.
Note 3 example
Evolutionary psychologists ran a simple test: men made far more impatient, short-term choices after viewing photos of attractive women than after viewing luxury goods. Present bias isn't random. It's triggered by cues our ancestors cared about, firing in a context that no longer rewards it.
From: In Defence of Behavioural Economics
A specific, slightly provocative study scene illustrates the article's thesis that biases are contextually triggered adaptations, not flaws.
