Note 1 pattern
Every product team I've worked with tracks what customers click. Almost none track what they abandoned and why. So you optimise the path people took while staying blind to the one they refused. More data, same blind spot.
From: Why Product Teams Misunderstand Their Customers
It names a recurring pattern (measuring completed behaviour, ignoring abandoned behaviour) and takes a clear argumentative stance.
Note 2 pattern
Nudges work best on problems that are immediate, personal, and tangible: pay your tax, save more, take the stairs. Climate change is none of those. It's abstract, distant, and systemic. The tool isn't broken. We've just been aiming it at the wrong scale.
From: From Page to Practice: The Power and Pitfalls of Applying Nudge Theory for Climate Action
It names the pattern behind where nudges succeed and fail, then reframes the failure as a mismatch of tool and scale, which is the article's core POV.
Note 3 pattern
The gym membership isn't a mistake. It's a purchase of identity. People pay in January to become the kind of person who goes, and the payment feels like the transformation. Watch for this everywhere: the course, the app, the planner. We buy the future self and skip the work.
From: February's Over. The Gym Is Empty Again.
It reframes January signups as identity-buying and names the recurring pattern across products, giving readers a lens they'll recognise in their own behaviour.
