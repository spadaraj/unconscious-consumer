Note 1 hook
Vinyl isn't nostalgia. People buying records grew up on streaming. What they're really paying for is friction: the flip side, the needle drop, the fact that you can't skip. Convenience removed the part of listening that made it feel like a choice.
From: Undercurrents: The Quiet Return of Analog in a Digital World
It reframes the analog revival as a rejection of frictionless design rather than sentimentality, which lands as counter-intuitive and stands alone.
Note 2 hook
Inflation doesn't end when prices stabilise. The fear rewires how you value money and stays put. You'll keep anchoring to the prices you remember long after they've stopped being real.
From: The Invisible Hand in Your Wallet: Unmasking the Impact of Inflation Fears on Your Spending Habits
Opens with a counter-intuitive claim (the effect outlasts the cause) that reframes inflation as psychological, not just economic.
Note 3 hook
Starbucks once pulled breakfast sandwiches off the menu, not because they sold badly, but because the cooking smell was killing the coffee aroma. They chose scent over food revenue. Your nose is doing more shopping than you think.
From: Hidden Influences: The Power of Scents and Sensibility in Retail
The counter-intuitive Starbucks decision reframes scent as a deliberate revenue trade-off, and the last line implicates the reader directly.
