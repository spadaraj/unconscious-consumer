## 2026-07-06 — AI outsourcing memory and encoding

As we can do more work faster with a capable assistant, taking the time to fully understand everything we're doing gets much harder. Work gets shipped, but the user may not have encoded all the information. The ideas the work is based on may not even have come fully from them. The result: people don't learn from what they've done, don't remember what they've done, can't speak to what they've done, or know how to build from what they've done. I'm observing this in myself and thinking through how to mitigate it while still using assistants. The mitigation I'm testing: writing one paragraph in my own words after any session, before closing the tab, explaining what I decided and why.

Framing rule: In Notes on this topic, position Adam as the diagnostician who noticed the phenomenon and has active mitigation practices. Not the case study. Avoid direct admissions that undermine his credibility on the current work (no "I couldn't tell if that was mine" without a clear "and here's how I catch it now" follow-up). Universalize where possible ("your internal state", "the temptation with an assistant") rather than autobiographising the loss.

Style rule for this topic: use descriptive alternatives ("assistant", "collaborator", "capable model") or specific tool names ("Claude", "ChatGPT") rather than the bare term "AI". Same aesthetic as CLAUDE.md's article-body rule.
